*Love.*

The word, unbidden, bloomed in my mind, and I felt my jaw tighten. It was absurd. Utterly, completely absurd. My mind, usually so meticulously organized, so reliant on logic and data, rebelled. "How in the world did you like someone in just 3 days?" I muttered to the empty air, the question a sharp, incredulous protest against this illogical invasion of my carefully constructed inner world. 

This wasn't how emotions worked. This wasn't how I worked. It felt funny, like a joke played on me, and profoundly uncomfortable, this sudden, irrational pull that defied all reason. The quiet hum of the night, which had been so comforting hours ago, now felt like a vast void, mirroring the unexpected chasm this revelation had opened inside me.

The sodium lamps of the deserted street cast long, fractured shadows across the damp pavement, but the dim light did nothing to dull the persistent, bewildering glow that had ignited within me. I loved her. The phrase echoed, an unwelcome guest, in my head. I shook my head, as if to dislodge it, but it clung. My first instinct, the one honed over years of detached observation and logical processing, was to dismantle it, to dissect this feeling until it revealed itself as something far less inconvenient: infatuation, curiosity, perhaps merely the lingering effect of unexpected companionship.


I walked back to the hostel, each step a reluctant march against this internal tide. The mud-stained bag felt heavier on my shoulder, not from its contents, but from the sudden, inexplicable weight of a feeling I didn't want to carry. The familiar path, which had promised solace earlier in the day, now felt like a stage for an internal argument. 

How could such a profound shift occur in just three days? It was against all my principles of slow, considered development, of keeping others at a comfortable, analytical distance. This was messy. This was... human. And for the first time, the thought of being merely human, subject to such sudden, illogical surges, filled me with a strange mix of dread and a curious, undeniable hint of something new. The crisp evening air, meant to clear my head, only seemed to sharpen the unwanted clarity of this bewildering truth.

I entered my room and put my bag on my untidy bed, stretching as I went to make myself a cup of tea. The chipped mug grew warm to the touch as I poured in the hot water from my kettle, looking outside at the empty bus shelter bench, waiting to see if she would come. Just her presence, just a slight smile from her, just a look at me--

*Crash*. The bag fell to the floor from the bed, disturbing my reverie. Good, I thought, I shouldn't be distracted by things like this.

I lay down on the bed, processing what I was thinking. My stomach felt weird, a dull discomfort radiated throughout me, almost painful.

"If pain is what love is, I don't want it", I said to myself, as flatly as possible. But I knew, deep down inside, that I was now a changed person. "Changed? Me? Never. Never. Ne-ve....", I murmured, losing my thoughts and speech to darkness.

Sunday morning was sunny too, as the day is named. But as clear as the day may have been, I wasn't. 

The sun's bright rays blinded me as I woke up. Yet I did not feel pain like I used to feel. But it wasn't that the pain had vanished, it had just shifted to a different place in the body.

I clutched at my heart once, blinked, and then looked out at the bus stop, sunshine reflecting off its acrylic roof. I suddenly felt a fleeting sense of embarrassment, and took my hand away from my chest. No, I couldn't possibly be that deep in this. No, I couldn't. I was logical. I was dedicating myself to my studies, only my studies, nothing else. 

The bus stop was empty.

I threw on an older pair of jeans, and walked out into the sunshine, harsh and oppressive (Keep in mind the dude is in Oregon). I shielded my eyes and walked down the sloping road, past the oak groves and apple trees, reaching the town.
